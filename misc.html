Other Math

These are some of the mathematical documents about “research”, from undergraduate years. I don’t know whether the claims have been proven elsewhere or earlier, and make no such claims!

  • Uniformly Distributed Dice Sums: This addresses the question of whether n dice, each with m sides, can be biased such that the probability distribution of their sum is the uniform distribution, and shows that this is not possible.

    The question was originally posed and answered by G. Chen, M. Rao, and W. Shreve in the article Can One Load a Set of Dice So That the Sum Is Uniformly Distributed?, Mathematics Magazine Volume 70, 1997. The above proof is different and simpler than the original, but also accomplishes less.

  • Transposition Distance of Permutations: This shows that the minimum number of transpositions required to go from one permutation to another is the rank of the difference of the corresponding permutation matrices.

    The question, claim, and proof were worked out jointly by myself and my classmate Sabareesh Ramachandran.
