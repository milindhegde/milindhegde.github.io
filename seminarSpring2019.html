Title and Abstracts

Graph limits and heuristics for LD on dense random graphs

Jacob Calvert

We will discuss—on a heuristic level—the large deviation principle for dense random graphs, introducing some objects from graph limit theory as necessary.

Notes prepared by Jacob are here.

Replica Symmetry in Large Deviations in Random Graphs

Onyebuchi Ekenta

The symmetric phase of a random graph distribution refers to the case where the distribution can be closely approximated with a simple Erdos-Renyi graph model G(n,p) with the appropriate choice of the parameter p. We will discuss the situations in which a random graph distribution conditioned on a large number of subgraphs enters and leave its symmetric phase.

Reference: arxiv.org/pdf/1210.7013.pdf

LDP upper bound in the weak topology

Milind Hegde

We will start by establishing some preliminaries of the proof of the LDP, namely that the limit of the \(G(n,p)\) graphon is the constant graphon \(p\) and that the proposed rate function is lower semi-continuous in the cut metric. Then we'll recall a theorem from general LD theory and apply it to establish the large deviations upper bound for weakly closed sets.

LDP upper and lower bound in the cut metric

Milind Hegde

We will extend last week's result on the LD upper bound for weakly closed sets to sets which are closed in the cut metric. Then we'll turn to proving the lower bound half of the LDP via a sort of change of measure trick as in the proof of Cramer's theorem from linear large deviations, and hence finish the proof of the LDP for dense random graphs.

Optimal transport

Efe Aras

I will be presenting a quick introduction to optimal transport and go over ways on how optimal transport helps prove certain inequalities regarding distances. In particular, we will first discuss Wasserstein distances, and then prove Kantarovich duality for Wasserstein distances, and then specialize to the case of W2. In particular, we will see how Fenchel duals naturally show up in the case of W2, and thus we get convex transport maps. We will then prove HWI and Bakry-Emery criterion for LSI (along with a minor discussion on log-concave distributions). I will go into as much rigor as there is in arxiv.org/pdf/1003.3852.pdf

(For those of you who want to know what I am planning to do, I will do theorems (1.5), (1.7), 2.2, 2.3, 2.7-2.9, 7.1-4 arxiv.org/pdf/1003.3852.pdf with parantheses on the first two to indicate I am not fully sure if I want to cover those)

Bounding Expected Suprema via the Generic Chaining

Milind Hegde

I will introduce the basic ideas behind "the generic chaining" of Talagrand, which is a general tool to bound the expected suprema of a class of stochastic processes. Along the way we'll see how (the easier-in-practice) Dudley's entropy bound is a weakening of it. While on a technical level the strategy is essentially just a clever union bound, the amazing fact is that the generic chaining bound is sharp upto a universal multiplicative constant - though we will not be able to prove this.
